1 · UK politics

Brexit campaigners launch Stay Out to fight a return to the EU

New today: The Stay Out campaign launched on Friday night, a week before the UK–EU summit.

A new campaign called Stay Out has launched against the UK rejoining the EU. It is chaired by Oliver Lewis, a former Brexit negotiator, and backed by Lord Gove, who chaired Vote Leave. It says rejoining would cost £39.3bn a year, a figure the BBC has not verified.

Why it matters: Andy Burnham says Brexit did more harm than good, but he has ruled out big changes before the next election. The campaign opens a new fight over Europe as he seeks closer ties.

What next: Burnham is expected to sign deals on food, energy and youth mobility at the UK–EU summit in Brussels on Friday 20 November.

Sources: BBC News (via Yahoo News)

2 · World

UK among five European countries named to join the Ukraine peace talks

European Council President António Costa said the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland will represent Europe in the next round of US-led talks. They will sit alongside EU diplomats. It follows talks by US envoys with Vladimir Putin in Moscow and Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin.

Why it matters: It gives Europe, including Britain, a direct say in a deal that will shape its own security. The Kremlin has not confirmed it will accept European negotiators.

What next: No date is set for the next round. The EU plans new sanctions on Russia on Monday, according to French minister Benjamin Haddad.

Sources: Al Jazeera, 13 November · Al Jazeera, 12 November · TheJournal.ie

3 · Immigration

Government approves an Oxfordshire army site to house 1,256 asylum seekers

The office of Communities Secretary Angela Rayner granted planning permission to turn the former MoD Bicester barracks, near Piddington, into housing for 1,256 asylum seekers. Villagers had protested, saying it would pose a threat to women in the area. The Home Office says a final decision to go ahead is yet to be taken.

Why it matters: The Home Office says it is closing asylum hotels and moving people to sites such as former military bases. The local MP, Calum Miller, says roads, GPs and other services will be strained.

What next: Opponents now have six weeks to challenge the decision in court.

Sources: ITV News Meridian

4 · World

Trump, Putin and Xi will hold their first three-way meeting in China this week

The White House said Donald Trump will meet Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping together on Thursday, at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in southern China. It will be their first three-way meeting. Trump wants to talk about cutting nuclear arsenals, and officials expect the Iran war to come up.

Why it matters: It is a rare chance for the three leaders to discuss nuclear weapons and the Iran war together. US officials say Ukraine will be handled in separate talks that do not include China.

What next: Trump leaves Washington on Monday and attends a summit in the Philippines on Tuesday before flying on to China.

Sources: CBS News
