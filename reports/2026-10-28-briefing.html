1 · Economy

Healey delivers his first Budget today with little room to spare

New today: The Budget is today, after a week of pre-Budget reporting; we have not reported its contents.

Chancellor John Healey is due to give his first Budget to MPs at about 12.30pm today. He is keeping Rachel Reeves's fiscal rule that day-to-day spending must be covered by taxes by 2029/30.

Why it matters: The Institute for Fiscal Studies says higher inflation and borrowing costs leave him a roughly £35bn gap to fill with tax rises, benefit cuts or smaller spending plans. Fuel bills, pensions and tax are the areas to watch.

What next: The Office for Budget Responsibility publishes its forecast alongside the speech. Treat pre-Budget reports as unconfirmed until the Chancellor has spoken.

Sources: Brit Brief on the IFS pre-Budget analysis · Which? on the Budget date and contents

2 · Medicine

Hospital consultants in England plan a 48-hour strike from 25 November

The British Medical Association says consultants will strike from 06:59 on 25 November to 06:59 on 27 November, with emergency cover only. It would be their first strike since 2023.

Why it matters: Planned operations and clinics that need a consultant would be hit, with 7.37 million treatments already waiting in England. The union wants shorter hours and better pay.

What next: The BMA says it will call the strike off if the Health Secretary commits to formal, funded negotiations before 25 November.

3 · UK politics

Peers take up the bill that would end the right to choose jury trial

The Courts and Tribunals Bill reached the House of Lords for second reading on 27 October. MPs passed it 301 to 193 after ministers dropped plans for judge-only trials but kept the end of a defendant's right to choose a jury in either-way cases.

Why it matters: The government wants to cut the Crown Court backlog. The Law Society and Bar Council say the change will not fix it and will damage fairness and trust.

What next: The Lords will examine the bill in detail at later stages. Reports say peers may try to amend the jury changes.

Sources: UK Parliament, Lords business · Law Society on the bill
