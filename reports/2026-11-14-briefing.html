Five stories worth knowing today, in plain English: what happened, why it matters and what comes next.

Noodle News desk · reading time ~3 min
1 · UK politics

Lords committee warns the EU deals would bring hundreds of EU laws a year

New today: A House of Lords committee published its report on following EU rules on Friday.

The House of Lords European Affairs Committee said the planned UK–EU deals would fundamentally change relations with the EU. Its chair, Lord Stirrup, said a food and farming deal alone could bring at least 780 pieces of EU law a year. Much of that would amend existing rules.

Why it matters: Under the deals the UK would follow EU rules on food, carbon pricing and electricity as they change. The committee says Parliament needs a new system to keep track of them.

What next: Andy Burnham is due to sign the deals at a UK–EU summit in Brussels on Friday 20 November.

Sources: UK Parliament: European Affairs Committee · Streamline Feed

2 · Medicine

Hospital consultants in England call off next week's strike to hold pay talks

The British Medical Association has paused the strike by consultants in England planned for 25 and 26 November. It said the government had agreed to formal negotiations on pay and working conditions. No offer has been made yet.

Why it matters: It lifts the threat of a two-day walkout by senior hospital doctors next week. The BMA says consultants' pay has fallen by about a quarter in real terms over 17 years.

What next: No start date for the talks has been given. The BMA says it will call new strikes if the talks do not make quick progress.

Sources: British Medical Association

3 · Society

Leaseholders in England will get new rights to challenge service charges from April

The government set out rules to make service charges clearer and easier to challenge. From 1 April 2027 leaseholders can ask for information on their charges, and will not have to pay their landlord's legal costs for challenging a bill. Andy Burnham said unfair charges were “simply wrong”.

Why it matters: The housing ministry says millions of leaseholders face fast-rising and often unclear bills. The changes are meant to make unfair increases easier to fight.

What next: The first changes start on 1 April 2027. Standard bills and a ban on passing on insurance commissions follow on 1 November 2027.

Sources: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government · CMS (law firm)

4 · AI

Jensen Huang quits as Nvidia's chief executive after a week of scandal

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, the company told US regulators, and finance chief Colette Kress is running the firm for now. Nvidia gave no reason. US newspapers had reported that Huang had affairs, including with a senior employee.

Why it matters: Nvidia's shares closed 4.3% lower the day he quit, after falling 27% from their October record. US lawmakers want answers over reports that intelligence officials suspected one woman of spying for China.

What next: Nvidia reports its quarterly results on 17 November. A US Senate committee has invited Huang to a hearing on 3 December.

Sources: Nvidia filing to the SEC · Newsweek

5 · World

UN chief calls for drastic emissions cuts as COP31 climate talks enter their last week

António Guterres called for an “immediate and drastic reduction” in emissions at the COP31 talks in Antalya, Turkey. Big oil and gas producers and users are resisting firm new targets. The United States has skipped the talks for a second year.

Why it matters: The talks decide how fast countries move away from the fuels that warm the planet. A UN report last month found the world is on course to pass 1.5C of warming.

What next: Countries' proposals close on Saturday. Australia, which leads the negotiations, aims to publish a draft deal early next week.

Sources: Reuters via Yahoo News
