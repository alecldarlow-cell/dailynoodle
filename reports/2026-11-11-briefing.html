The briefing: Wednesday 11 November

Four stories worth knowing today, in plain English: what happened, why it matters and what comes next.

Noodle News desk · reading time ~2 min
1 · World

US moves military aircraft out of RAF Mildenhall after a reported Iranian threat

The US Air Force moved aircraft out of RAF Mildenhall in eastern England on Monday because of a threat, a US official said. Open-source trackers saw 13 aircraft leave for Scotland, Spain and Germany. The Wall Street Journal reported that the threat was linked to Iran.

Why it matters: It is the second US-run base in Britain to move aircraft out in under two months, after bombers left RAF Fairford over a suspected Iran-linked plot. Downing Street says British air bases are safe.

What next: A US official said the move was a precaution and the planes may return. Two men are charged under the National Security Act over Fairford.

Sources: ABC News (Associated Press) · The National

2 · World

Russia and Ukraine open their first US-brokered peace talks since February

New today: The talks opened on Tuesday. Russia's lead negotiator said he hoped they would help both sides advance towards peace.

Russian, Ukrainian and US negotiators met in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. The White House said the talks would focus on security guarantees for Ukraine. Ukraine's Rustem Umerov first met US envoys and officials from the UK, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the EU.

Why it matters: Land is still the main gap: Vladimir Putin wants Ukraine out of four regions, and Kyiv refuses to give up territory. Three earlier rounds this year brought no significant breakthrough.

What next: The talks end on Wednesday 11 November. US envoy Jared Kushner says talks between Europe and Russia should follow.

Sources: NPR, via MPR News

3 · UK politics

UK and EU ministers agree steps on citizens' rights and trade before the summit

UK minister Hamish Falconer and European Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič met in Brussels on Tuesday. They oversee the Brexit Withdrawal Agreement. They adopted decisions on social security, AI and forced labour, and a declaration on parcel fees in Northern Ireland.

Why it matters: The agreement protects about 6 million EU citizens in the UK and 1 million UK nationals in the EU. Both sides want a smooth move from temporary to permanent residence during 2027.

What next: Leaders meet in Brussels on 20 November. Conservatives attack a leaked EU draft keeping fishing access until 2038; ministers call it unagreed.

Sources: GOV.UK joint statement · AOL UK, on the fishing row

4 · Science

World leaders begin speeches at the COP31 climate summit in Turkey

Speeches by 42 leaders, including Brazil's Lula and Ursula von der Leyen, begin on Wednesday in Antalya. On Tuesday, countries launched a pledge to raise electricity's share of energy use from about 23% to 35% by 2035.

Why it matters: Climate Action Tracker says current policies reach only 28% by then. UN chief António Guterres said leaving fossil fuels should no longer be treated as "taboo or intractable".

What next: The UK's Andy Burnham opens the leaders' speeches on Thursday 12 November.

Sources: Climate Home News
