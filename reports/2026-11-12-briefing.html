1 · World

Russia and Ukraine agree a big prisoner swap as Abu Dhabi talks end with no peace deal

New today: The talks have ended: both sides agreed a prisoner exchange, but not the terms of peace.

Two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi ended on Wednesday with a deal to exchange prisoners. There was no breakthrough on ending the war. US envoy Jared Kushner called it “a very tough meeting but a productive one”.

Why it matters: Land is the hardest issue, Kushner said. Russia’s chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said “every inch of our land” will return to Russia, so the two sides remain far apart.

What next: Kushner and Steve Witkoff will brief Putin in Moscow, then meet Zelensky. European leaders meet in Dublin on Thursday, where Burnham is expected to announce more Russia sanctions.

Sources: Al Jazeera · The Moscow Times · PA via Kent Online

2 · World

US charges three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot to attack RAF Fairford

New today: New US charges over the September incident at Fairford, after reports that US aircraft left RAF Mildenhall.

US prosecutors charged three men with terrorism offences on Tuesday. They say the men worked for Iran’s Revolutionary Guards and an Iraqi militia. One is accused of having vans, some carrying fuel barrels, driven to the base, which the US used for bombers in the Iran war.

Why it matters: A US prosecutor said the threat from Iran to America’s allies “is very real”. Two men already face UK charges under the National Security Act over the same incident.

What next: Iran denies any role. It says the Strait of Hormuz will stay shut until the US meets its conditions for talks.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera

3 · Society

Minister says limits on gagging clauses could go further after Walliams allegations

The Guardian and ITV News named David Walliams on Tuesday as the subject of allegations of sexual misconduct, after he sought an injunction to stop their reports. He denies any wrongdoing. Equalities minister Bridget Phillipson said the government should “look to go further” on non-disclosure agreements.

Why it matters: Non-disclosure agreements and injunctions can stop people speaking out about harassment. Phillipson said she believes wealthy men misuse injunctions to silence women.

What next: A consultation on further limits to non-disclosure agreements is under way. The Ministry of Justice is looking at how to stop injunctions being misused.

Sources: PA via Kent Online

4 · Science

Burnham names a climate security envoy in his speech to the COP31 summit in Turkey

New today: The Prime Minister spoke at the summit and named a new climate security envoy.

Andy Burnham told world leaders in Antalya that “the climate crisis is upon all of us now”. He appointed Dame Madeleine Alessandri, a former chair of the Joint Intelligence Committee, as special representative for climate security. He also restated the UK’s 2050 net zero goal.

Why it matters: Her job is to treat climate and nature risks as threats to national security. Green groups say the UK’s promises will ring hollow if ministers approve the Rosebank oilfield.

What next: The Energy Secretary has yet to decide on Rosebank. A decision on the target for electric car sales is also pending.

Sources: PA via Kent Online
