The briefing: Tuesday 10 November

Four stories worth knowing today, in plain English: what happened, why it matters and what comes next.

Noodle News desk · reading time ~2 min
1 · Society

Russell Brand jailed for ten and a half years for rape and sexual assaults

A judge at Southwark Crown Court jailed Russell Brand for 10 years and six months on Monday. A jury convicted him last week of one rape and three sexual assaults on four women. He was cleared of two other rape charges and had denied all the allegations.

Why it matters: Judge Joel Bennathan said Brand showed a "casual and arrogant disregard" for women. The case has pushed ministers to look again at rape trials, which matters to every victim deciding whether to report.

What next: Brand must serve about seven years before release. Two further allegations are still under investigation.

Sources: Yahoo News, with Reuters and AP · ITV News · Irish News (PA)

2 · Society

Burnham declines to back the police watchdog fully over the Henry Nowak case

Henry Nowak, an 18-year-old student, was murdered by Vickrum Digwa. His family say Hampshire police first suggested Henry was the aggressor. They saw bodycam footage of his arrest only on the first day of the trial.

Why it matters: On Monday the Prime Minister would not give the Independent Office for Police Conduct his full confidence, saying the family "have been let down". It puts the body that holds police to account under review.

What next: Former Lord Chancellor Alex Chalk will lead an independent review. The watchdog's own investigation is due early next year.

Sources: ITV News

3 · World

Russia and Ukraine hold US-brokered peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the first since February

Russian, Ukrainian and US negotiators begin two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky leads Russia's team and Rustem Umerov leads Ukraine's. Officials from the UK, France, Germany and Italy will also be in Abu Dhabi.

Why it matters: Land is the main block: Russia still demands all of Donbas and Ukraine refuses. Any deal would shape Europe's security, though Russia opposes European participation in the talks.

What next: The talks run until Wednesday 11 November. G7 foreign ministers meet in Paris, with Ukraine's foreign minister joining on Tuesday.

Sources: Reuters, via Yahoo News · Xinhua

4 · Medicine

Minister says hospital consultants in England will not be paid more to stop strike

Health minister Karin Smyth said on Monday that consultants will not be paid more money. She said talks are instead about improving their working lives. Consultants in England plan to strike on 25 and 26 November over pay and conditions.

Why it matters: The walkout would hit hospitals at the start of what Smyth called a "difficult winter". Emergency care will still be covered, with a Christmas Day-style service.

What next: The two-day strike starts on 25 November. Smyth urged the British Medical Association to call it off.
